# Audience checklists

Use as a starting point when the reader falls into one of these groups. Each is a default to confirm with the user, not a rule; the user's stated preferences always override.

## Executive / leader (decides resources and direction)

- **Wants:** the answer, the size of it, what to decide. Usually skims.
- **Lead with:** headline + the one number that proves it + the decision being asked for.
- **Show:** trend vs. target or prior period; where the problem is concentrated; cost or risk of inaction.
- **Cut:** item-level or row-level detail, method, anything they can't act on at their level. Put it in an appendix.
- **Watch:** giving a leader too many "priorities" (they'll pick none); claiming causes you can't support; naming individuals who report to them unless asked.

## Practitioner / operator (acts on it day to day: teachers, analysts, team leads)

- **Wants:** specifics they can use: which items, which topics, which segments. Will read closely.
- **Lead with:** the headline, then quickly to the detail that changes what they do on Monday.
- **Show:** concrete examples, the weakest and strongest specifics, enough context to recognise the situation.
- **Tone:** data first. Professionals in their own domain often resent being told what to do; ask whether they want recommendations or just the evidence. Plain language over statistical jargon, with the same underlying numbers.
- **Watch:** prescribing instead of informing; implying blame for a result; showing more individuals than their role entitles them to.

## External / public (families, customers, board, press)

- **Wants:** what happened, whether it matters to them, what is being done. Usually non-fluent with the data.
- **Lead with:** the plain-language headline and the context that keeps it honest (conditions, purpose, what the measure can and can't say).
- **Show:** few numbers, each with a comparison they can feel; aggregate rather than individual; progress and next steps alongside any low number.
- **Tone:** truthful without being alarming or defensive. State difficult conditions plainly instead of softening them away; readers who find out later lose trust.
- **Watch:** leaderboards and named lists (get explicit approval, and consider showing rank without scores); any detail that could identify a person in a small group; jargon; the document being forwarded far beyond its intended readers.

## Affected individuals (a person reading about themselves)

- **Wants:** their own result, in context, and what to do next.
- **Show:** their position relative to a meaningful reference, strengths before gaps, specific next steps.
- **Watch:** exposing other individuals' data; unexplained labels that read as a verdict; comparisons that feel like a ranking unless the user wants that.

## Mixed audience

Don't write one document that tries to serve everyone. Produce one spine per audience from the same findings, keep the facts identical, and vary selection, detail, tone and naming per the checks above.
